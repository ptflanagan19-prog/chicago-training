Phase structure

  • Base (Weeks 1–4): Rebuild aerobic engine from your current 15–20 mpw base. Introduce threshold and track work carefully.
  • Build (Weeks 5–9): VO2max intervals, longer tempos, two fitness benchmarks. This is where sub-1:30 fitness is made.
  • Race-Specific (Weeks 10–13): HM-pace intervals, long threshold runs, 15-mile peak long run. Simulate race conditions.
  • Taper (Weeks 14–16): Reduce volume, preserve intensity. Arrive at the start line fresh and sharp.

About your paces

Starting paces are intentionally conservative — built from your 1:37 half, 3:55 marathon, and 20:10 5K. You are not expected to run at goal HM pace in training until late in the plan. After the Week 5 time trial, adjust all zones. The training works if the easy runs are truly easy.

Pace update schedule

  • Week 5 (5K time trial): If you run sub-19:30, shift all zones 10–15 sec/mi faster. Sub-19:00 → shift 20+ sec/mi.
  • Week 9 (10K tune-up): A 10K around 44:00–45:00 confirms sub-1:30 is achievable. Update threshold and HM pace zones.
  • Week 13 (fitness check): 5×1200m at goal HM pace. If RPE is 7–8 (not 9+), you're ready.

Heart rate guidance

  • Easy runs: Genuinely conversational — full sentences. If you're breathing hard, slow down. HR is a check, not a target.
  • Threshold: Comfortably hard. You can say 3–4 words, not a sentence. "Controlled discomfort."
  • Intervals: Hard, mouth-breathing effort. Recovery between reps should drop HR below ~140 bpm.
  • Long runs: Start at easy pace. Last 20–30% can drift toward marathon pace. Never force it.

The most important rule

Easy days must be easy. Most runners sabotage their training by running easy days 30–45 sec/mi too fast. If you nail this one habit, the hard days will be harder and the fitness gains will be larger. The easy pace range above is wide on purpose — err toward the slow end, especially early in the plan.

Periodization by phase

  • Weeks 1–4 (Base): 3×10–12 reps, moderate load. Learn the movements, build the foundation. Form over weight.
  • Weeks 5–9 (Build): 4×6–8 reps, heavier load. Strength phase. Cut squats/RDL to 2 sets in the heaviest running weeks.
  • Weeks 10–13 (Race-Specific): 3×8, maintain strength. Reduce heavy lower body within 48h of quality runs.
  • Weeks 14–16 (Taper): 2×6–8, lighter weights. Keep neuromuscular activation without adding fatigue.

Scheduling rules

  • Never do heavy squats or deadlifts the day before a hard track session (Tuesday). If Monday strength is very heavy, shift it to after the easy Wednesday run.
  • Calf raises are non-negotiable every week — the most important injury-prevention exercise for runners. Don't skip them.
  • If you're very sore from lifting, the strength session was too heavy. Back off 10–15% and build more gradually.
  • Hip abduction work (Session A) and Copenhagen planks (Session B) protect against IT band syndrome and hip injuries — keep these even in taper.

Race morning protocol

  • Wake 3 hours before gun time. Eat familiar carb meal (oatmeal, banana, toast) — ~400–500 cal.
  • Sip 16–20 oz water over the morning. Stop drinking 30 min before start.
  • Arrive at start 45 min early. 10-min easy jog warm-up + 4×20 sec strides at HM goal pace.
  • Take a gel 15 min before start if using them. Second gel at mile 7. Practice this in training.
  • Nothing new on race day — shoes, socks, nutrition, all tested in long runs beforehand.

Execution keys

  • Miles 1–2 will feel easy. That is correct. Resist every urge to speed up. Bank nothing — the race is miles 9–13.
  • Don't watch overall pace — watch current mile pace. React lap by lap. A single 6:35 mile in the first 5 can cost you the race.
  • Miles 9–10 are where sub-1:30 is won or lost. Every successful finish requires a strong 9th mile.
  • If you're 10 seconds ahead of pace at the half (mile 6.55), stay disciplined. Do not accelerate.
  • Use the crowd at miles 12–13. Visualize catching runners ahead. The last mile is mental, not physical.

Taper week reminders

  • Mileage drops ~40% in Week 14, ~50% in Week 15. Legs will feel weird — flat, heavy, or strangely fresh. All normal.
  • Do NOT drop intensity during taper. Keep the short speed sessions. The volume drops, the quality stays.
  • Sleep Thursday–Saturday nights before the race is the single highest-leverage recovery tool available to you.
  • Trust the training. You will arrive at the start line having done the work. Race the plan, not the crowd.
